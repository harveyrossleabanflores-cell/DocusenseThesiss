1. In September 1975, there were two assassination attempts on President Gerald Ford in a span of 17 days. The first occurred when Lynette "Squeaky" Fromme aimed a pistol at Ford in Sacramento, California. The second incident occurred in San Francisco when he was shot by Sara Jane Moore. Thankfully, both attempts were unsuccessful, and Ford lived. 

2. Prior to the second attempt, there were warning indicators. Shortly prior to the attack, Sara Jane Moore had already drawn the attention of law enforcement and was being held with a firearm. But at the time, she wasn't seen as an immediate, serious threat. This demonstrates the need to thoroughly assess and share information about suspicious people.

3. Agents from the Secret Service surrounded and remained close to the President. The attacker was stopped from firing during the initial attempt by an agent who grabbed her right away. On the second attempt, Ford was swiftly moved by Secret Service agents into his car and away from the danger.

4. The Secret Service's prompt response proved to be highly effective. When the weapons were exposed, the agents were close enough to react right away. They were able to stop both attacks from turning into successful assassinations by acting quickly and evacuating President Ford.

5. The case demonstrates that ongoing threat assessment, close protection, appropriate screening, and prompt evacuation protocols are essential components of VIP security. People who have previously displayed suspicious behavior or tried to bring weapons close to a protected VIP should also be watched by security personnel.

2ND CASE

1. On September 6, 1901, President William McKinley was assassinated while in Buffalo, New York, for the Pan-American Exposition. Leon Czolgosz approached him while he was extending greetings to members of the public, concealing a pistol under a handkerchief. Then Czolgosz fired two close-range shots at McKinley. Later, McKinley passed away from his wounds.

2. Threats against political leaders and anarchist violence were worries at the time. The possibility of attacks against the President was known to security officials. Czolgosz, however, managed to get through the reception line and get close to McKinley without being recognized as a significant threat.

3. McKinley was guarded by soldiers, police, detectives, and members of the Secret Service. The line of people approaching the President was managed by security guards stationed throughout the reception area.

4. Following the shooting, the security staff responded right away. Czolgosz was apprehended and restrained right away. Additionally, the President received medical care and was taken to receive treatment. The security team's prompt action stopped the attacker from doing more damage even though they were unable to stop the initial attack.

5. The assassination proved that when a VIP is in close proximity to a sizable crowd, having security guards nearby is insufficient. Better screening, restricted access, keeping a safe distance between the VIP and the general public, and more robust threat assessment are all important components of VIP protection. The assassination of McKinley also led to modifications in presidential security, such as the creation of ongoing Secret Service protection.
